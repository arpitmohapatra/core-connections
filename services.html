Pelvic Health Physiotherapy

Individualized pelvic health physiotherapy for women, men and 2SLGBTQIA+ individuals. We believe great pelvic health care begins with feeling heard, understood and supported — in a safe, inclusive and welcoming environment where you can ask questions, explore your concerns and take an active role in your care.

Read more about pelvic health care

Our approach is integrated, individualized and collaborative. We combine clinical expertise, skilled hands-on treatment, movement and education to understand the interconnected factors contributing to your symptoms and help you move toward meaningful, lasting improvements in your pelvic health and well-being.

What sets our care apart

  • Inclusive care: we welcome people of all ethnicities, gender identities, gender expressions and sexual orientations.
  • Clinical experience: our pelvic health physiotherapists bring up to 19 years of clinical experience.
  • Trauma-informed care: all our physiotherapists are trained in trauma-informed care, prioritizing choice, consent, clear communication and your sense of safety.
  • Gender-affirming care: several team members have advanced training to support people navigating gender-affirming procedures and practices, including pelvic pain, post-operative vaginoplasty rehabilitation, sexual function and dysfunction, and binding, tucking and other gender-affirming practices.
  • Comfort: our treatment rooms are private and beautiful.

An internal vaginal and/or anorectal assessment is only ever done when clinically appropriate, and it is always your choice: you may consent, defer or decline at any time.

Pre & Postnatal Physiotherapy

We’re passionate about supporting you throughout pregnancy, preparing you for labour and birth, and helping you recover with confidence postpartum. Our integrated pelvic health and orthopedic approach considers the whole person, helping you address concerns, understand your body and prepare for the journey ahead.

Read more: prenatal physiotherapy

Prenatal assessment

Your assessment is individualized to your needs and goals and may include screening for:

  • Pregnancy-related pelvic girdle, back, hip, neck and shoulder pain
  • Abdominal muscle separation (diastasis recti)
  • Pelvic organ prolapse symptoms and urinary incontinence
  • Pelvic floor muscle tension, weakness or coordination difficulties
  • Scar tissue from previous perineal tears or episiotomies

We also assess breathing and pressure management, abdominal and pelvic floor function, posture and movement patterns. Assessments are available in person or virtually at any stage of pregnancy. When clinically appropriate, an in-person assessment may include an internal pelvic floor examination, which is always optional and requires your informed consent.

Prenatal services

  • Pelvic health and orthopedic physiotherapy
  • Individualized exercise programs and Physioyoga
  • Breathing, core and pelvic floor rehabilitation
  • Hands-on manual therapy and scar mobilization
  • Bladder and bowel health education
  • Labour and birth preparation, including breathing, movement and birthing positions
  • Pregnancy and postpartum exercise guidance, including return-to-running planning
  • Urban poling, hypopressives and yoga ball therapy
Read more: postnatal physiotherapy

We’re passionate about supporting your recovery after birth, helping you reconnect with your body and return to the activities you love. Your assessment is tailored to your birth experience, current concerns and recovery goals.

Postnatal assessment may include

  • Pelvic girdle, lower back, neck, shoulder and upper back pain
  • Abdominal muscle separation (diastasis recti)
  • Pelvic organ prolapse symptoms and urinary incontinence
  • Pelvic floor and abdominal muscle tension, weakness and coordination
  • Scar mobility or sensitivity following perineal tears, episiotomy or caesarean birth
  • Musculoskeletal concerns related to breastfeeding or chestfeeding

We also look at breathing and pressure management, posture and movement patterns, and your ability to manage everyday activities such as lifting, carrying and feeding your baby.

Postnatal treatment may include

  • Pelvic floor rehabilitation: improving muscle awareness, relaxation, coordination and strength.
  • Core and abdominal rehabilitation: restoring coordinated function between the diaphragm, abdominal muscles and pelvic floor.
  • Hands-on manual therapy and scar mobilization, when appropriate.
  • Breathing and pressure management to support coordinated movement and everyday activities.
  • Functional movement and strengthening: progressive exercise tailored to your recovery and goals.
  • Postpartum education: bladder and bowel health, posture, body mechanics, babywearing and feeding positions.
  • Return-to-exercise guidance: individualized progression toward running, sport and other activities.
  • Pessary fitting: for symptoms of pelvic organ prolapse or urinary incontinence, when appropriate — now offered through the Ottawa Pessary Clinic.

Our goal is to help you better understand your body, rebuild strength and confidence, and feel supported in your postpartum recovery. Pre & postnatal conditions we treat.

Orthopedic Manual & Sports Physiotherapy

Orthopedic manual physiotherapy combines skilled hands-on assessment and treatment with education and individualized exercise to address musculoskeletal pain, mobility limitations and movement difficulties. Our FCAMPT-certified physiotherapists bring advanced post-graduate training and up to 36 years of clinical experience.

Read more about orthopedic care

Our integrated approach considers how joints, muscles, fascia, nerves and movement patterns interact to influence symptoms and function. We work collaboratively with you to identify factors contributing to your concerns and develop a treatment plan that supports recovery, improves function and helps you return to the activities that matter to you.

FCAMPT-certified physiotherapists

FCAMPT-certified physiotherapists have completed rigorous post-graduate training and examination, with an emphasis on advanced assessment skills and clinical reasoning to help distinguish between potential sources of pain and movement dysfunction. Learn more about the qualification through the Canadian Academy of Manipulative Physiotherapy (CAMPT).

Your assessment may include

  • Health and symptom history: exploring your concerns, daily activities and goals.
  • Posture and movement assessment: functional movement, body mechanics and relevant ergonomic factors.
  • Hands-on examination: relevant joints, muscles, fascia and nerves.
  • Clinical testing: differentiating between potential contributing factors to guide treatment.
  • Collaborative planning: an individualized approach based on your findings.

Your treatment plan may include

  • Manual therapy for pain and mobility restrictions.
  • Exercise and movement retraining for strength, mobility, coordination and stability in daily activities, work and sport.
  • Breathing, posture and motor control training.
  • Pain science and nervous system education for persistent pain and self-management.
  • Education and injury prevention to reduce the risk of recurrence.

Additional training and approaches

  • Integrated Systems Model: Diane Lee
  • Advanced orthopedic assessment and manual therapy
  • Shoulder rehabilitation: Lyn Watson
  • Cervical spine assessment and rehabilitation
  • Hip stability training: David Lindsay
  • Vestibular rehabilitation: dizziness, vertigo and balance
  • Dry needling and acupuncture
  • Exercise programming and functional movement retraining
  • Mindful movement, yoga and Swiss ball exercise
  • Yoga Tune Up® and Urban Poling
  • Return-to-running and sport-specific rehabilitation

Orthopedic conditions we treat

Specialty

Ehlers-Danlos Syndrome (EDS) & Hypermobility Spectrum Disorders (HSD)

EDS and HSD are connective tissue conditions that can affect joint stability, movement, pain and everyday function. Symptoms and their impact vary considerably from person to person. Physiotherapy can help improve strength, joint control, confidence with movement and participation in daily activities.

Read more about EDS & HSD

Hypermobile EDS (hEDS) is the most common subtype of EDS, a group of heritable connective tissue disorders. HSD describes symptomatic joint hypermobility that does not meet the diagnostic criteria for hEDS or another specific connective tissue disorder.

Common concerns

  • Joint hypermobility, pain and instability
  • Recurrent sprains, strains or joint dislocations
  • Reduced joint control and body awareness
  • Fatigue, muscle fatigue and reduced exercise tolerance
  • Difficulty with daily activities or physical exercise

How can physiotherapy help?

Treatment is individualized to your symptoms, abilities and goals, with careful consideration of joint instability, pain and fatigue, and may include:

  • Strengthening and joint stability: gradual, tailored exercises for support and control around hypermobile joints.
  • Proprioception and movement retraining: body awareness, balance and coordination.
  • Pain management: strategies to manage pain and sensitivity, with gentle hands-on techniques when appropriate and tolerated.
  • Joint protection and activity modification.
  • Pacing and exercise progression: balancing activity and recovery.
  • Postural and functional training for everyday tasks, work, exercise and sport.
  • Scar and soft-tissue care: gentle, individualized techniques when scar sensitivity or restricted tissue mobility contributes to symptoms.

Together, we develop a practical, adaptable treatment plan to help you move with greater confidence, manage symptoms and work toward the activities that matter most to you.

Vestibular Physiotherapy

Vestibular physiotherapy helps assess and treat dizziness, vertigo and balance difficulties. Through individualized treatment, education and exercise, we aim to improve your stability, reduce symptoms and help you return to the activities you enjoy with greater confidence.

Read more about vestibular care

Symptoms we treat

  • Dizziness, vertigo or lightheadedness
  • Balance problems, unsteadiness or falls
  • Symptoms triggered by head movements or changes in position
  • Blurred vision or difficulty focusing while moving
  • Persistent dizziness following a concussion or other injury
  • Sensitivity to busy environments, movement or visually complex surroundings
  • Difficulty walking on uneven surfaces or in low light

Dizziness can also arise from medical conditions, medication effects or changes in blood pressure. Your physiotherapist will screen for concerns requiring medical assessment and refer you to an appropriate healthcare professional when indicated.

Your assessment may include

  • A thorough history and screening for medical red flags
  • Assessment of head and eye movements
  • Balance, coordination and walking assessment
  • Specific tests to help distinguish between potential causes of your symptoms
  • Assessment of the neck, shoulders and upper back, when indicated
  • Blood pressure testing in different positions, if appropriate
  • Education and an individualized home exercise program

Some assessment procedures may temporarily reproduce dizziness, nausea or other symptoms. Your physiotherapist will explain the process, monitor your response and adapt the assessment to support your safety and comfort.

Your treatment plan may include

  • Canalith repositioning manoeuvres for BPPV.
  • Gaze stabilization exercises.
  • Balance and coordination training.
  • Motion sensitivity exercises.
  • Neck physiotherapy: manual therapy, exercise and other appropriate techniques.
  • Strengthening and conditioning.
  • Education and self-management.
Vestibular FAQ

How long does it take to improve?

Recovery depends on the underlying cause, symptom duration and individual circumstances. Some cases of BPPV improve within one to three treatment sessions, while other conditions may require several weeks or months of rehabilitation.

What is the difference between dizziness and vertigo?

Dizziness is a broad term that can describe lightheadedness, unsteadiness, disorientation or a floating sensation. Vertigo is the sensation that you or your surroundings are moving when they are not — for example, feeling that the room is spinning or rocking.

Can the neck or inner ear cause dizziness?

Yes. The vestibular system in the inner ear helps your brain interpret movement and maintain balance, and conditions affecting it can cause dizziness, vertigo, visual disturbance and unsteadiness. Neck pain or injury may also contribute in some people. Vestibular physiotherapists assess potential vestibular, musculoskeletal and other contributing factors and recommend medical assessment when appropriate.

Will dizziness improve on its own?

Some causes improve over time, and BPPV may sometimes resolve spontaneously. However, symptoms can persist or recur. Assessment can help identify whether targeted treatment, rehabilitation or medical follow-up is appropriate.

Can I drive after my appointment?

Please arrange for someone to accompany you and take you home after your vestibular assessment or treatment. Testing and treatment can temporarily increase dizziness, nausea or unsteadiness, and we cannot predict your response in advance. We recommend arranging a ride with someone you know rather than planning to drive yourself or travel alone by public transit or rideshare.

How can a vestibular physiotherapist help?

A physiotherapist with vestibular training can assess your symptoms, identify findings that may help explain your difficulties, develop an appropriate treatment plan and refer you to another healthcare professional when further medical assessment is needed.

Physiotherapy for Adults 60+

Staying active and maintaining independence are important at every stage of life. Our experienced physiotherapists provide compassionate, individualized care to help you manage pain, maintain mobility, improve strength and balance, and continue doing the things you love.

Read more: staying strong, active & independent

Our integrated approach combines skilled hands-on treatment, functional movement retraining, exercise and education to address the factors contributing to your symptoms and support your long-term health and well-being.

Conditions we treat

  • Arthritis, pain & stiffness: hands-on treatment, individualized exercise and education.
  • Bone health (osteopenia & osteoporosis): appropriate strengthening, balance and weight-bearing exercise.
  • Orthopedic conditions & injuries: back, sacroiliac joint, hip, knee and shoulder conditions, nerve-related symptoms and persistent musculoskeletal pain.
  • Balance & fall prevention: balance training, functional exercise and movement strategies.
  • Dizziness & vestibular conditions.
  • Pelvic health: urinary incontinence, pelvic organ prolapse and other concerns, including pessary fitting when appropriate (see the Ottawa Pessary Clinic).

Your care may include

  • Advanced orthopedic manual therapy from FCAMPT-certified physiotherapists
  • Hands-on treatment: manual therapy and soft-tissue techniques, when appropriate
  • Individualized exercise: progressive programs for strength, mobility, balance and functional capacity
  • Movement retraining and education
  • Vestibular physiotherapy
  • Pelvic health physiotherapy
  • Urban Poling: a walking-based exercise option to support mobility, balance and physical activity

Our goal is to help you maintain your strength, confidence and independence so you can continue participating in the activities that matter most to you.

Pelvic Floor Biofeedback

Visual or auditory feedback that helps you understand how your pelvic floor muscles are working in real time.

Read more

By translating muscle activity into cues on a screen or device, biofeedback can help you develop greater awareness of your ability to contract, relax and coordinate these muscles. It may be incorporated into pelvic health physiotherapy when you have difficulty sensing or coordinating your pelvic floor muscles.

  • Pelvic floor muscle awareness — recognizing when you contract or relax.
  • Pelvic pain & muscle tension — practising relaxation of muscles that tend to stay tense.
  • Urinary incontinence — practising contractions and coordination.
  • Pelvic organ prolapse — may support strengthening and coordination when appropriate.
  • Bowel dysfunction — practising relaxation and coordination during bowel movements.
  • Postpartum & post-surgical rehabilitation.

Biofeedback is one of several tools that may be used, and can be combined with individualized exercises, education, movement retraining and hands-on physiotherapy.

Cupping Therapy

Cups placed on the skin create gentle suction, used as an adjunct to manual therapy.

Read more

The suction gently lifts the skin and underlying tissues and may be used to address muscle discomfort, restricted movement and areas of scar tissue. It may be considered as part of an individualized plan for scar mobility (including abdominal and caesarean-section scars), muscle and joint discomfort, movement and flexibility, and localized muscle tension.

Cupping is not suitable for everyone and may cause temporary skin redness, bruising or tenderness. Your physiotherapist will discuss its suitability and your preferences before treatment. It is an optional component of care; its benefits vary, and it does not replace active rehabilitation when that is indicated.

Hypopressives for Pelvic Health

Specific breathing techniques combined with coordinated postures and movements.

Read more

The approach emphasizes postural control, breathing awareness and coordination of the deep abdominal and pelvic floor muscles. Hypopressive exercises typically involve a controlled exhalation followed by a brief breath hold and an expansion of the rib cage.

They may be considered as one component of a broader program, and may be explored in relation to pelvic organ prolapse symptoms, urinary incontinence, diastasis recti, pelvic floor coordination, postpartum recovery, and postural control. Research on effectiveness for specific pelvic health conditions is still developing, and they may not be appropriate or beneficial for everyone. We begin with a pelvic health assessment to determine whether this approach is suitable for you.

Important: hypopressives may be unsuitable in certain circumstances, including pregnancy and some cardiovascular or blood pressure conditions. Your physiotherapist will review precautions before recommending them.

Rectal Balloon Therapy

Sensory and coordination training for selected bowel and pelvic floor conditions.

Read more

It uses a small balloon attached to a thin catheter to help you practise recognizing rectal sensations and coordinating the muscles involved in bowel movements. When appropriate, a trained pelvic health physiotherapist gently inserts a lubricated balloon into the rectum and gradually inflates it. The procedure is explained beforehand, and your consent and comfort guide each step. You may decline or stop at any time.

It may be considered for difficulty emptying the bowels, chronic constipation, altered rectal sensation and, in selected cases, fecal incontinence. A session may include sensory awareness, sensory retraining, muscle coordination and balloon expulsion, and can be combined with bowel habit education, toileting strategies and pelvic floor retraining.

Therapeutic Ultrasound

High-frequency sound waves delivered through a handheld treatment head, considered selectively.

Read more

Ultrasound can produce thermal (continuous) or non-thermal (pulsed) effects in the tissues. It has been used in physiotherapy as an adjunctive treatment for selected musculoskeletal conditions; evidence for its benefits varies by condition, and it is not a necessary part of most rehabilitation programs.

Your physiotherapist will consider your symptoms, goals and the available evidence. If appropriate, it may be used alongside more active approaches such as individualized exercise, movement retraining and education. We emphasize treatments that are appropriate to your needs and support your functional goals.
