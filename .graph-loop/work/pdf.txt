--- p34: Ehlers-Danlos Syndrome (EDS) & Hypermobility Spectrum Disorders (HSD) Ehlers-Danlos syndromes (EDS) and Hypermobility Spectrum Disorders (HSD) are connective tissue conditions that can affect joint stability, movement, pain and everyday function. Symptoms and their impact vary considerably from person to person. Ehlers-Danlos Syndromes (EDS) EDS is a group of heritable connective tissue disorders associated with differences in tissue structure and function. Hypermobile EDS (hEDS) is the most common subtype. Other subtypes have distinct features and may involve skin fragility, impaired wound healing or, in certain cases, blood vessel and organ complications. Common concerns may include: ● Joint hypermobility and instability ● Joint and muscle pain ● Recurrent sprains, strains or joint dislocations ● Fatigue and reduced exercise tolerance Hypermobility Spectrum Disorders (HSD) HSD describes symptomatic joint hypermobility that does not meet the diagnostic criteria for hEDS or another specific connective tissue disorder. Symptoms can overlap with those of hEDS, and the impact on daily life can be equally significant. Common concerns may include: ● Joint pain and instability ● Recurrent sprains or soft-tissue injuries ● Reduced joint control and body awareness ● Muscle fatigue and reduced exercise tolerance
